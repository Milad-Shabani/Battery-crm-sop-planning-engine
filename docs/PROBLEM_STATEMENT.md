# Problem Statement

## Context

**Pars Voltage Battery Industries Co.** is a mid-size lead-acid battery
manufacturer running four production lines (automotive SLI, motorcycle,
industrial/UPS, and deep-cycle/solar) and roughly 180 employees. Its go-to-
market is entirely B2B: ~70 accounts — distributors, retailers, OEM/fleet
buyers, and export customers — placing recurring orders, managed through a
Microsoft Dynamics-365-style CRM (accounts, contacts, leads, an opportunity
pipeline with sales stages, service cases).

Two things make this planning problem genuinely different from a
consumer-goods forecasting problem:

1. **Demand is lumpy, not smooth.** A handful of large B2B orders can swing
   a week's total by 10x. Weekly, even monthly, statistical forecasting is
   only moderately accurate on its own (see the backtest results in
   `README.md`) — there's real, irreducible variance from individual deal
   timing that no amount of tuning a time-series model fixes.
2. **The CRM already knows about some of that variance.** A specific
   opportunity sitting in "3-Propose" with a contractual expected close
   date next month is concrete, probability-weighted evidence a pure
   history-based forecast has no way to see. Ignoring it and forecasting
   from history alone throws away real information sitting one system
   away.

There's also a genuine seasonal driver, independent of the CRM: lead-acid
battery capacity drops sharply in cold weather, and a marginal battery that
survived autumn often fails outright on the first hard freeze — automotive
and motorcycle battery replacement demand spikes every December-January.

## Goal

Build a **CRM-integrated S&OP planning engine**: blend statistical demand
history with the open sales pipeline into a single forecast, turn that into
a capacity- and labor-constrained production plan and material requirements
plan, and surface the CRM funnel health (conversion, win rate, sales cycle,
at-risk accounts) that a sales manager needs alongside it — all published
to a polished Excel workbook and a live dashboard, not just a database
table.

Concretely, the pipeline must:

- **Forecast monthly demand per production line** from order history, with
  a real backtested accuracy number — including an honest account of how
  much lumpy B2B demand limits pure statistical accuracy.
- **Blend in the open CRM opportunity pipeline** as a forecast overlay:
  every open opportunity's `estimated_quantity x probability` lands in the
  month bucket matching its expected close date, added on top of the
  statistical baseline for that specific product.
- **Allocate production across 4 lines under two simultaneous constraints**
  — machine-hours and labor-hours — prioritizing which products get made
  when the two don't add up to full demand.
- **Explode the plan into material requirements via a bill of materials**,
  net against current inventory, and recommend purchase orders sized and
  timed to each material's supplier lead time and safety-stock policy.
- **Report CRM funnel health**: lead conversion by source, win rate by
  account segment, sales cycle length (won vs. lost), lost-opportunity
  reasons, a sales rep leaderboard, and a simple, explainable at-risk
  account flag (open case + no recent order).
- **Publish a real business deliverable**: a multi-sheet Excel workbook
  with native charts and conditional formatting — the artifact a CRM/ops
  team would actually open — plus a live HTML dashboard that reads from
  that same workbook.
- **Run entirely on synthetic but realistic data**, generated by this repo,
  so the whole pipeline can be run and inspected with zero external
  dependencies or real customer data.

## Non-goals

- Real-time CRM sync — this reads a point-in-time export of the pipeline,
  not a live Dynamics 365 connection.
- Point-in-time pipeline snapshots — the dataset carries each
  opportunity's *current* state, not its full stage-change history, so the
  backtest necessarily measures the statistical component alone (see
  `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`).
- Multi-line product flexibility — each SKU is produced on one primary
  line in this model.

## Expected output

A `fact_sop_monthly_summary` table (forecast vs. planned units, fill rate,
revenue captured/at risk, materials below safety stock) plus supporting
detail (`fact_demand_forecast`, `fact_production_plan`,
`fact_material_requirements`, `fact_forecast_accuracy`) and CRM analytics
(`fact_lead_funnel`, `fact_win_rate_by_segment`, `fact_rep_leaderboard`,
`fact_at_risk_accounts`) — written to SQLite, Parquet, **and** a formatted
Excel workbook (`SOP_CRM_Report.xlsx`) with native charts, which the HTML
dashboard reads from directly.
